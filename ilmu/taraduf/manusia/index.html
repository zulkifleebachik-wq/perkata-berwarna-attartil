ManusiaHuman beings

Keluarga makna · 3 kalimah al-QuranMeaning family · 3 Qur’anic words

Dalam bahasa Melayu kita sebut 'manusia' sahaja. Al-Quran memakai tiga perkataan: insan (manusia), bashar (manusia dari sudut tubuh zahirnya) dan nas (orang ramai). Al-Raghib al-Asfahani menyatakan bahawa al-Quran memakai bashar di tempat yang mengambil kira jasad dan kulit zahir manusia. Bagi insan, beliau menukil beberapa pendapat tentang asal namanya, antaranya kerana manusia saling berjinak dan kerana manusia lupa. Abu Hilal al-'Askari pula menyatakan bashar membawa makna rupa yang elok atau sesuatu yang zahir, manakala nas dikaitkan dengan gerak.In Malay we simply say 'manusia'. The Qur'an uses three words: insan (human being), bashar (human being viewed as outward body) and nas (people). Al-Raghib al-Isfahani states that the Qur'an uses bashar where the body and outward skin of man are in view. For insan, he reports several views on the origin of the name, among them that humans are sociable with one another and that humans forget. Abu Hilal al-'Askari states that bashar carries the sense of fine appearance or of being outwardly visible, while nas is linked to movement.

Al-Raghib menukil, dengan kata qila (ada yang berkata), beberapa pendapat tentang asal nama insan (manusia). Satu pendapat: manusia dicipta dalam keadaan tidak dapat hidup tanpa berjinak sesama mereka. Pendapat lain: asal katanya insiyan (bentuk asal yang dikaitkan dengan lupa), kerana Allah berpesan kepadanya lalu dia lupa. Al-Raghib menyampaikan kedua-duanya sebagai nukilan, tanpa memilih salah satu.Al-Raghib reports, with qila (it has been said), several views on the origin of the name insan (human being). One view: man was created unable to live without being sociable with his own kind. Another view: the word's original form is insiyan (original form linked to forgetting), because God entrusted him with a command and he forgot. Al-Raghib conveys both as reports, without choosing either.

Al-Raghib menyatakan bahawa al-Quran mengkhususkan kata bashar (manusia dari sudut tubuh zahirnya) bagi setiap tempat yang mengambil kira jasad dan zahir manusia. Beliau memberi contoh al-Furqan 25:54, tentang Allah yang menciptakan bashar daripada air.Al-Raghib states that the Qur'an reserves the word bashar (human being viewed as outward body) for every place where man's body and outward form are in view. He gives the example of al-Furqan 25:54, about God who created bashar from water.

وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ مِنَ ٱلْمَآءِ بَشَرًا فَجَعَلَهُۥ نَسَبًا وَصِهْرًا وَكَانَ رَبُّكَ قَدِيرًا

al-Raghib al-Asfahani, al-Mufradat (المفردات في غريب القرآن), h. 828 · Shamela ↗al-Raghib al-Isfahani, al-Mufradat (المفردات في غريب القرآن), p. 828 · Shamela ↗

Al-Raghib menukil tiga pendapat tentang asal kata nas (orang ramai), semuanya dengan kata qila (ada yang berkata). Pertama, asalnya unas (bentuk asal bagi nas), lalu huruf awalnya dibuang apabila ditambah alif lam. Kedua, ia terbalikan daripada nasiya (lupa), dengan bentuk asal insiyan (bentuk asal yang dikaitkan dengan lupa). Ketiga, ia daripada nasa yanusu (bergerak-gerak). Ketiga-tiga pendapat dilaporkan tanpa diutamakan.Al-Raghib reports three views on the origin of the word nas (people), all with qila (it has been said). First, its origin is unas (original form of nas), whose first letter was dropped when alif lam was added. Second, it is a transposition of nasiya (to forget), with the original form insiyan (original form linked to forgetting). Third, it comes from nasa yanusu (to sway, move about). All three views are reported without preference.

bashar البشر ↔ nas الناس

«أن قولنا البشر يقتضي حسن الهيئة وذلك أنه مشتق من البشارة وهي حسن الهيئة»

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, Mu'jam al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (معجم الفروق اللغوية), h. 101 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, Mu'jam al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (معجم الفروق اللغوية), p. 101 · Shamela ↗

«وَيجوز أَن يُقَال إِن قَوْلنَا بشر يَقْتَضِي الظُّهُور وَسموا بشرا لظُهُور شَأْنهمْ وَمِنْه قيل لظَاهِر الْجلد بشره وَقَوْلنَا النَّاس يَقْتَضِي النوس وَهُوَ الْحَرَكَة»

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), h. 276 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), p. 276 · Shamela ↗

Abu Hilal al-'Askari menyatakan bahawa kata bashar (manusia dari sudut tubuh zahirnya) membawa makna rupa yang elok. Pada beliau, ia diambil daripada bisharah (keelokan rupa). Beliau menambah satu kemungkinan lain: bashar membawa makna zahir, kerana keadaan manusia itu nyata, dan kulit luar dinamakan basharah (kulit luar). Kata nas (orang ramai) pula, menurut beliau, membawa makna naws (gerak).Abu Hilal al-'Askari states that the word bashar (human being viewed as outward body) carries the sense of fine appearance. In his view, it is derived from bisharah (fineness of appearance). He adds another possibility: bashar carries the sense of being visible, because man's state is manifest, and the outer skin is called basharah (outer skin). The word nas (people), according to him, carries the sense of naws (movement).

Muhammad Salim, same entry.

al-Raghib al-Asfahani, al-Mufradat (المفردات في غريب القرآن), h. 124 · Shamela ↗al-Raghib al-Isfahani, al-Mufradat (المفردات في غريب القرآن), p. 124 · Shamela ↗

Al-Raghib menyatakan bahawa insan (manusia) disebut bashar (manusia dari sudut tubuh zahirnya) kerana kulitnya nyata, tidak ditutupi bulu. Ini berbeza dengan haiwan yang ditutupi bulu biri-biri, rambut atau bulu unta. Beliau juga menyatakan al-Quran memakai bashar di setiap tempat yang mengambil kira jasad dan zahir manusia.Al-Raghib states that insan (human being) is called bashar (human being viewed as outward body) because his skin is visible, not covered with hair. This differs from animals covered with wool, hair or camel fur. He also states that the Qur'an uses bashar in every place where man's body and outward form are in view.
