TakutFear

Keluarga makna · 7 kalimah al-QuranMeaning family · 7 Qur’anic words

Dalam bahasa Melayu kita biasa menyebut «takut» sahaja. Al-Quran pula menggunakan beberapa akar kata untuk perasaan ini, dan setiap satu membawa warna makna sendiri. Kata yang paling umum ialah khauf (takut). Menurut al-Raghib al-Asfahani, khasyyah (sejenis rasa takut) ialah takut yang bercampur rasa mengagungkan dan selalunya lahir daripada ilmu. Menurut Abu Hilal al-'Askari, rahbah (rasa takut) ialah takut yang berpanjangan, faza' (rasa takut yang datang mengejut) ialah takut yang datang secara tiba-tiba, dan hadhar (sikap berjaga-jaga) ialah usaha mengelak bahaya. Al-Raghib menerangkan ru'b (rasa gerun yang memenuhi hati) sebagai keadaan terputus kerana hati dipenuhi takut. Tentang wajal (gementar hati) dan isyfaq (rasa bimbang bercampur prihatin), ulama berbeza pandangan: al-Raghib mengaitkan kedua-duanya dengan takut, tetapi al-'Askari berkata kedua-duanya bukan daripada jenis takut. Huraian ini diambil daripada al-Raghib, al-'Askari dan Ibn Faris, serta Ibn al-Jawzi dalam ilmu makna-makna kata al-Quran.Malay usually has just «takut». The Qur'an uses several roots for this feeling, each with its own shade. The most general word is khawf (fear). According to al-Raghib al-Isfahani, khashyah (a kind of fear) is fear mingled with reverence and usually born of knowledge. According to Abu Hilal al-'Askari, rahbah (fear) is prolonged fear, faza' (sudden fright) is fear that strikes suddenly, and hadhar (wariness) is guarding against harm. Al-Raghib explains ru'b (terror filling the heart) as being cut off because the heart is filled with fear. On wajal (trembling of the heart) and ishfaq (anxious care) the scholars differ: al-Raghib ties both to fear, but al-'Askari says neither is a kind of fear. These explanations come from al-Raghib, al-'Askari and Ibn Faris, and from Ibn al-Jawzi in the science of the Qur'an's word-senses.

He also sees a difference in how the verbs are used: one says «فزعت منه» with «من» (from), but «خفته» directly. 'I feared him' means he himself is what I fear, while 'I took fright from him' means he is where my fright began, since «من» marks a starting point.

Hadhar dapat menolak bahaya, sedangkan khauf (takut) tidak dapat menolaknya. Kerana itu orang berkata «خذ حذرك» (ambillah langkah berjaga-jaga), tetapi tidak berkata «خذ خوفك». Hadhar can ward off harm, whereas khawf (fear) cannot. That is why one says «خذ حذرك» (take your precautions) but not «خذ خوفك». (AtTartil's note: hadhar is not made a member of this family because it is an act of caution, not the feeling of fear; it appears here because al-'Askari contrasts it with khawf.)
