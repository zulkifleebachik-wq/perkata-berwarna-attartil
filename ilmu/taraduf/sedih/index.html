SedihSorrow

Keluarga makna · 7 kalimah al-QuranMeaning family · 7 Qur’anic words

Dalam bahasa Melayu kita biasa berkata «sedih» atau «dukacita». Al-Quran pula memakai beberapa akar kata untuk perasaan ini, dan setiap satu menyorot sisi yang berlainan. Menurut Abu Hilal al-'Askari, ghamm (kesedihan yang menyelubungi hati) ialah keadaan hati yang mengecut; huzn (dukacita) ialah ghamm yang telah menebal; karb (kesusahan hati) ialah ghamm yang tebal disertai dada yang sempit; hasrah (sesal yang memedihkan) ialah ghamm yang berulang kerana sesuatu kebaikan terlepas; asaf (kesedihan yang amat sangat) ialah hasrah yang bercampur marah; dan bathth (kesedihan yang diluahkan) ialah kesedihan yang tidak lagi dapat disimpan. Ada pula kazim (yang memendam rasa di dalam dada), iaitu orang yang menahan dukacitanya. Al-Raghib al-Asfahani, Ibn Faris, al-Fairuzabadi, Ibn al-Jawzi dan Ibn 'Ashur menambah huraian masing-masing; ada kalanya mereka berbeza pendapat, seperti Ibn 'Ashur tentang bathth. Kisah Nabi Ya'qub dalam surah Yusuf (12:84 dan 12:86) menghimpunkan empat daripada kata-kata ini.In Malay we usually say «sedih» or «dukacita». The Qur'an uses several roots for this feeling, each lighting a different side. According to Abu Hilal al-'Askari, ghamm (sorrow) is the heart contracting; huzn (grief) is ghamm grown thick; karb (distress) is thick ghamm with a tightened chest; hasrah (painful regret) is ghamm that keeps returning over a lost good; asaf (intense grief) is hasrah mixed with anger; and bathth (grief poured out) is sorrow that can no longer be held in. There is also kazim (one who keeps his feelings pressed down inside), one who holds his grief in. Al-Raghib al-Isfahani, Ibn Faris, al-Fairuzabadi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn 'Ashur add their own explanations; at times they differ, as Ibn 'Ashur does on bathth. The story of Prophet Ya'qub in surah Yusuf (12:84 and 12:86) brings four of these words together.

Dikecualikan juga perkataan kazimin (orang-orang yang menahan) di Ali 'Imran 3:134, yang bermaksud menahan marah, dan di Ghafir 40:18, tentang hati yang sampai ke kerongkong pada hari kiamat.Only the two places that clearly mean holding grief in are counted: kazim (one who keeps his feelings pressed down inside) at Yusuf 12:84 and makzum (one choked with held-in feeling) at al-Qalam 68:48, since Ibn 'Ashur explains both as 'grieving'. Excluded: kazim at al-Nahl 16:58 and al-Zukhruf 43:17, since Ibn 'Ashur explains 16:58 as 'angry, full of rage' (he also refers back to Yusuf 12:84 there), and Basmeih renders both verses as 'holding in his anger'; 43:17 is the same scene. Also excluded is kazimin (those who hold in) at Al 'Imran 3:134, meaning restraining anger, and at Ghafir 40:18, about hearts at the throats on the Last Day.

Tentang Yusuf 12:84, Ibn 'Ashur memberi dua kemungkinan. Pertama, kazim (yang memendam rasa di dalam dada) ialah bentuk mubalaghah (melebih-lebihkan atau menguatkan makna) bagi kata 'yang menahan': Nabi Ya'qub menahan dukacitanya, tidak menzahirkannya di hadapan orang, dan menangis ketika bersendirian. First, kazim (one who keeps his feelings pressed down inside) is a mubalaghah (intensive form) of one who holds in: Prophet Ya'qub held his grief in, did not show it before people, and wept in private. Second, it means 'grief-stricken', like makzum (choked with held-in feeling) at al-Qalam 68:48. Basmeih's Malay rendering of this ayah reads 'suppressing his anger'. That reading is also found in the tafsir works: al-Baydawi (Anwar al-Tanzil, 3/174) explains kazim here as one filled with anger at his sons, holding it in his heart and not showing it. huzn الحزن ↔ karb الكرب

«أَنَّ الْحُزْنَ تَكَاثُفُ الْغَمِّ وَغِلَظُهُ مَأْخُوذَةٌ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ الْحَزْنِ وَهُوَ الْغَلِيظُ الصُّلْبُ وَالْكَرْبَ تَكَاثُفُ الْغَمِّ مَعَ ضِيقِ الصَّدْرِ وَلِهَذَا يُقَالُ لِلْيَوْمِ الْحَارِّ يَوْمُ كَرْبٍ أَيْ كُرِبَ مَنْ فِيهِ»

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), h. 267 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), p. 267 · Shamela ↗

Abu Hilal al-'Askari menerangkan bahawa huzn (dukacita) ialah ghamm (kesedihan yang menyelubungi hati) yang telah menebal dan menjadi kasar; namanya diambil daripada hazn (tanah yang keras dan kasar). Karb (kesusahan hati) pula ialah ghamm yang menebal disertai dayq al-sadr (sempit dada). Kerana itu hari yang sangat panas disebut 'hari karb', kerana orang di dalamnya berasa sesak. Daripada huraian ini juga jelas hubungan huzn dengan ghamm: huzn ialah ghamm yang sudah tebal.Abu Hilal al-'Askari explains that huzn (grief) is ghamm (sorrow) that has thickened and grown coarse; the name comes from hazn (hard, rugged ground). Karb (distress) is thickened ghamm together with dayq al-sadr (tightness of the chest). That is why a scorching day is called 'a day of karb': those in it feel stifled. This also shows how huzn relates to ghamm: huzn is ghamm grown thick.

«أَنَّ الْحَسْرَةَ غَمٌّ يَتَجَدَّدُ لِفَوْتِ فَائِدَةٍ فَلَيْسَ كُلُّ غَمٍّ حَسْرَةً»

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), h. 267 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), p. 267 · Shamela ↗

Menurut al-'Askari, hasrah (sesal yang memedihkan) ialah ghamm (kesedihan yang menyelubungi hati) yang datang berulang-ulang kerana terlepasnya sesuatu kebaikan. Maka setiap hasrah ialah ghamm, tetapi tidak setiap ghamm ialah hasrah.According to al-'Askari, hasrah (painful regret) is ghamm (sorrow) that keeps renewing itself over a benefit that has slipped away. So every hasrah is ghamm, but not every ghamm is hasrah.

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), h. 267 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), p. 267 · Shamela ↗

Dalam fasal yang sama, Abu Hilal al-'Askari berkata asaf (kesedihan yang amat sangat) ialah hasrah (sesal yang memedihkan) yang disertai ghadab (kemarahan) atau ghayz (marah yang memuncak). Kemudian penggunaannya meluas hingga dipakai untuk makna marah sahaja, seperti az-Zukhruf 43:55, «Setelah mereka menyebabkan kemurkaan Kami». Kerana itu asaf berada di sempadan keluarga makna «sedih» dan «marah».In the same section al-'Askari says asaf (intense grief) is hasrah (painful regret) accompanied by ghadab (anger) or ghayz (seething rage). Its use then widened until it was used for anger alone, as in al-Zukhruf 43:55, 'when they angered Us'. So asaf sits on the border between the families «sedih» (sorrow) and «marah» (anger).

«وَقَوْلُنَا الْبَثُّ يُفِيدُ أَنَّهُ يَنْبَثُّ وَلَا يَنْكَتِمُ … وَأَصْلُ الْكَلِمَةِ كَثْرَةُ التَّفْرِيقِ … فَعَطَفَ الْبَثَّ عَلَى الْحُزْنِ لِمَا بَيْنَهُمَا مِنَ الْفَرْقِ فِي الْمَعْنَى»

Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), h. 267 · Shamela ↗Abu Hilal al-'Askari, al-Furuq al-Lughawiyyah (الفروق اللغوية), p. 267 · Shamela ↗

Al-'Askari menerangkan bahawa huzn (dukacita) menunjukkan beratnya hamm (kerisauan), manakala bathth (kesedihan yang diluahkan) menunjukkan kesedihan itu tersebar keluar dan tidak lagi tersembunyi; asalnya ialah menaburkan, seperti «kelkatu yang terbang berkeliaran» (al-Qari'ah 101:4). Dalam Yusuf 12:86 bathth disebut bersama huzn kerana kedua-duanya berlainan makna.Al-'Askari explains that huzn (grief) conveys the heaviness of hamm (worry), while bathth (grief poured out) conveys that it spreads out and is no longer hidden; its root sense is scattering, as in 'scattered moths' (al-Qari'ah 101:4). In Yusuf 12:86 bathth is joined to huzn because the two differ in meaning.
